INSPECT THE DESIGN

Two R7 frame ends carry the laptop on drop-in pegs, and a splice plate at the middle, registered by a 2 mm fin in the gap and epoxied on, receives an exchangeable seat-and-fence contact through the same fence channel. Each end grows a split plenum with a 120 mm extraction fan on its leaning face. Front and rear ties join the ends. Everything is printed except the fans and, optionally, four M4 screws per fan.

Exploded positions separate the two modules along the dock and then move each part along its own assembly direction; they illustrate, they do not simulate removal paths. The laptop and fans are reference bodies.

01 / CONTACT

One frame, swappable contact.

Each cradle end is the R7 frame: base, brace, a deck with two 22 mm channels along the docking axis, the 8° lid rail and a 6 mm outer wall. Everything else the laptop touches is a drop-in peg, a seat peg per machine and a fence peg per end, locked by one horizontal pin with a 0.15 mm cam offset. Another laptop needs new pegs, not a new dock.

P5 notes and fit corrections
02 / COOLING

Plenums pull, fans discharge.

The laptop stands hinge-down. Its rear exhaust fires into a 21 mm slot in each plenum roof; the fans lower the pressure there and throw the air out the lid side 18° above the desk. The underside intake stays open. Fans sit in a pocket against a flange and are held by four pins and a standoff guard, or by M4 screws through the bonus pilots.

Plates and estimates
03 / ASSEMBLY

Pins and keys, no screws.

Twenty-two printed pins and twenty flat keys join the ten major parts: round 7.9 mm shafts with a bed flat in 8.4 mm bores, keys with 1.8 mm of barb interference. Ties join the ends through floor-backed T joints. Support gussets under the socket bosses and seam tabs are additive only; no mating surface moved.

What has been printed, and what we learned
D9 P5: 59 printed parts, nine verified Orca plates, both plenums pass the closed-intake audit, no nominal interference, sampled pin, key and peg motions clear.
