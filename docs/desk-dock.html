Exploded positions explain the assembly; they do not simulate removal paths. Hand access, fits and fastening still need a physical prototype.

01 / SUPPORT

Feet carry the load.

Profiled end seats pass laptop weight into the shell perimeter and its integral feet. Separate ribbed bottom covers close the duct. Together they use about 94 g of PETG, 48% less than D7. The loading end and underside intake remain clear.

02 / COOLING

Fans belong inside the body.

Flat grilles slide into open-top rails. Separate clips retain the fan and grille; their leaves print in the layer plane. The pocket allows for the selected 120 × 120 × 25 mm fan and configurable pad thickness. The suction datum and 18° upward discharge stay fixed.

03 / ASSEMBLY

Align it. Remove it whole.

The connector slides ±3 mm in Y and −4 to +5 mm in Z. Its independent X stop sets engagement. Remove two shell mounting locks, pull 4.3 mm to release the keys, then withdraw the entire module outboard. The printed face-cam spring still lets the holder fold clear.

Review assembly and print planning
D8 prototype: 83 CAD solids and 43 production print meshes. Generic slicing estimates 1.66 kg of PETG including supports and brims; budget two 1 kg spools for the first build. Final P1S support removal, fit, strength, spring creep, release force, stability, cooling and noise still require qualification.
