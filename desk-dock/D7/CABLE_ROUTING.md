# Cable routing — lay in, then close the cover

Each bottom cover now has a grooved saddle at its outer end. Lowering the cover opens the passage along its edge. Lay the cable in the groove and close the cover around it; the electrical connector never passes through a small finished hole.

The provisional cable bundle envelope is **6 × 4 mm**, inside a **7 × 5 mm** rounded tunnel with clearance. “Pinch closed” describes the two parts closing around the cable, not squeezing its insulation. The saddle grows from the existing cover and stays within the dock's outer endwall. It adds no loose printed part and remains above the desk-foot plane.

Two integrated zip-tie bridges inside each outer endwall keep the wiring on the stationary shell. The lower bridge provides strain relief beside the exit; the higher bridge guides the lead along the wall. The **3.2 × 1.8 mm** rounded slots are intended for approximately **2.5 mm** ties. Tighten a tie only enough to retain the cable without deforming it. Keep the tie head and trimmed end away from the tunnel, panel flange and air passage.

The 120 x 120 x 25 mm fan pocket has a provisional lower-corner wire relief spanning the full frame depth. It is cut through the combined pocket, guide rail and duct wall, so underlying material cannot close it again. The relief opens toward the fan suction side and connects to the lower duct. Its 7 mm width allows the provisional 6 x 4 mm lead envelope at different positions along the thicker frame; actual fan clocking and lead position still need checking. The fan's 0.5 mm perimeter fit clearance is not a wire route. Leave a service loop or disconnect the fan inside the opened duct before lifting it out of its top-opening pocket.

The intended route is: actual fan frame exit → lower corner relief → a service loop against the cavity perimeter → shell-mounted tie bridges → split cover-edge tunnel. The two fans may need different clocking to suit their actual lead positions. Wiring is deliberately not modeled as a confirmed harness: the fan model, lead length, connector size, insulation, bend radius and tie-head envelope still need checking on the selected hardware.

These lugs serve the **fan leads only**. Keep a separate loose service loop in the USB cable. Do not attach that cable or its loop to the fan-wire lugs or tie it across the plug support's planned breakaway joint: that would tether the moving cassette and alter the release force. Check the complete released support motion with the real USB cable before setting a breakaway load.

To remove a cover, unload and lift the dock, release its hand locks, and lower the cover. Wiring and ties stay attached to the shell; the edge groove opens around the existing lead. Terminate any perimeter sealing tape beside the saddle rather than across the cable groove.

Nominal geometry checks should include the cable envelope through the complete assembled passage, a sideways placement path with the cover removed, cover lowering, body/cover intersections, guard removal, laptop travel, rubber-foot keepouts and the final airflow cavity. The geometry does not establish electrical suitability, physical strain relief, tie durability or fan-wire fit.
